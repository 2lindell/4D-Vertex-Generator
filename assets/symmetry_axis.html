Around a 2400-symmetry axis

The shapes round one of the axes of the extra half-turns (the 2400-element group's elements outside the swirlprism group), layer by layer out from the axis, each shown once and exploded so each stands apart from its neighbours. The axis is the purple line; the view is gnomonic, as in the domain page, centred on the axis.

drag to turn · scroll to zoom · hover a shape
  • Every named shape is shown once: all 55 walls, 40 regions, 24 edges and 5 corners. They come in layers out from the axis. The first layer is the wall X38, the region X68 on its sides, X68's other wall X99, and their edges (E1, C2a, B1) and corners (A1, C6). Each further layer is the shapes the shape map puts beside the last layer whose names are not shown yet. Four layers reach every name; the slider shows the first few only.
  • Each shape is shown once, with only the extent its seeds need once. Of the copies of a shape, one is shown, picked one shape at a time: first the copy that bounds, or lies on, a shape already shown round an edge they share (so the shapes round an edge follow on one from the next: round B1, X38, X68, X99, X61, F4, X49, F1, F3), then the copy beside most shapes shown, then the one nearest the axis. A shape a symmetry of the 2400-element group carries onto itself is cut to a part no symmetry repeats: X38, C2a, B1 and F3 by the axis's half-turn (each its half on the same side of the axis), and X16, C2b, X1, X12, X30, X7, X19 and X78 by other half-turns. (D1, D2, D3 and C3 also have a second symmetry, but it turns each about itself, holding it point by point, so they are shown whole.) Hover a shape to see what cut it and how many of its copies were met.
  • The view is exploded so that every shape keeps its place against its neighbours. The corners spread out as if the whole picture were scaled up about the axis, so each sits beyond the ends of its edges. Every other shape is set off the shapes on its boundary: an edge off its corners along itself (so C2a slides out from C6), the walls and regions round an edge off it in the directions they truly lie in from it, in their true order and over the arc they truly span (round E1 they span 311°, round B1 187°, round C2a 55°), opened out only where neighbours crowd: to at least 8° apart, or up to 30° round an edge with few shapes, the shapes of the earliest layer round it keeping their true directions and the rest swinging out from them. Each wall or region is also turned about the edge it opens out most round (of its own layer or nearer the axis), so the shapes round an edge fan out from it rather than standing parallel, like a deck of cards; a region off its walls towards its own volume. A shape bounded by several takes the mean of their placements. Moves pass only upward, corner to edge to wall to region, so no chain of shapes can carry one far from where it truly lies. Hover a shape for its name and why it has the extent shown. Walls and edges are solid; a region is shown by its shell, see-through.
