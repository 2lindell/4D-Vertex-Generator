Swirlprism cell atlas

One tetrahedral cell of the 600-cell, split by the horizontal H4 mirror β₃ = β₄ (the shaded plane on top). The half below it holds one copy of every seed; seeds in the splitting mirror are also drawn at their half-turn copy there. Points are seeds with golden-integer barycentric weights and every uniform H4 seed; each is coloured by the isogonal polychoron it produces. Shapes in the wiki's list are numbered in the wiki's own order.

Hover a point for its shape, golden weights and seed; click it to pin those details here and copy the seed. Click a row in the key to show only that shape; click it again to show everything.

Key

Counts are symmetry classes of cells, faces and edges, written the way the wiki writes them. The example seed can be pasted into the generator's Coordinates mode.

How to read the atlas

  • The tetrahedron is one 600-cell cell in gnomonic projection, so great circles are straight lines and mirrors are planes. The main ring runs along the vertical edge V₃V₄; the shaded top is the splitting mirror β₃ = β₄. The picture is drawn upside down so that plane is on top.
  • The half-turn that fixes the cell swaps V₁↔V₂ and V₃↔V₄, so the other half repeats this one exactly. This half cannot be halved again: the vertical mirror β₁ = β₂ does not preserve the symmetry, and the two quarters it separates hold different shapes.
  • The letter gives the vertex count and whether the wiki names the shape: A 120 named, B 240 named, C 600 named, D 600 unnamed, E 1200 named, F 1200 unnamed, T 1200 transitional, each numbered in the wiki's order. Shapes not in the wiki list are Y (600) and X (1200), numbered by how many classes of cells they have.
  • Marker shape shows the vertex count: circles 1200, diamonds 600, squares 120 or 240. Within each vertex count every wiki shape has its own colour, so shapes that share a region never share a style. Unlisted shapes are grey so they do not compete; click one in the key to pick it out.
  • Large outlined markers are uniform H4 seeds. They are labelled by the isogonal they produce; the uniform polytope is named only when that piece is an unlisted shape.
  • The light green line is an order-3 ghost girdle: the axis of an order-3 rotation of the 3600-element group (the swirlprism group with its right-hand 20 quaternions widened to 60). Seeds on it give X12 polytopes with 3600 symmetries; it is one of Bowers' 20 such girdles. Groups beyond that add no further lines.
  • Light purple lines are the axes of the extra half-turns. Three come from a family of 150 such circles; the one from the β₂ = β₃ cross ring to C2b is one of Bowers' 30 ghost girdles with skew 20-gonal symmetry. The swirlprism group (1200 rotations) and the extra half-turn Q together make a group of 2400 rotations; it has no mirrors, so it adds no planes, only these four axis segments. A seed on an axis is its own copy, and its polytope has 2400 symmetries; the exact icosafold points (✕) lie on them. Every other point has exactly one congruent copy elsewhere in the half-cell.
  • "2400 fundamental domain" shows one fundamental domain of the 2400-element group: the points closer to a centre on the E2 line, β ∝ (2, 2, 2, 1) (black dot), than to any of its 2399 images (a Dirichlet domain). Centres on the E2 line are the only ones whose domain stays inside the half-cell and holds every light purple axis on its surface. It has 13 corners and 10 faces and half the half-cell's volume. Four faces lie on the half-cell's own walls: the amber pair on β₁ = 0 and β₂ = 0 (glued to each other, as the half-cell's faces are) and two slate faces, on the fold and on β₄ = 0, each folding across a cross ring. The other six cut the half-cell in two: four light purple faces, each folding onto itself across a light purple axis, and a teal pair glued to each other by the extra half-turn's coset.
  • With "regular prism / antiprism cells" on: pink lines are where the pentagonal prisms are regular (square sides). Prisms only exist on the faces β₂ = 0 and β₁ = 0 and the mirror β₁ = β₂; there the regular ones lie on straight lines from the 600-cell corner: β₃ = β₁ + β₄ and β₃ = β₂ + β₄ (F4, ending at spidrox) and β₁ = β₂ = (β₃ − β₄)/φ (F1). Brown lines are where the pentagonal antiprisms are regular; they are straight too: β₁ = φ²β₂, β₃ = β₁ + β₄ (F1) and β₂ = φ²β₁, β₃ = β₂ + β₄ (E1), both from the 600-cell corner to the face β₄ = 0; β₂ = β₄, β₃ = β₄/(2φ²) from the corner (1, 0, 0, 0) to the E2 line (F3, F2, F1); and a short piece inside X32. Dashed: copies under the extra half-turn.
  • The grey line under "Where" says where a shape lives, from small nudges of its deepest sample: "fills a region", or the wall (plane) or line it lies on, or "a point or a curve" when no simple golden plane holds it. X shapes that only live on a wall, line or point are listed separately as transitional, grouped by where they lie. A shape can also appear on another wall or face (F4 also lies on the face β₂ = 0).
  • ✕ marks exact special points that are not golden points, in the colour of the shape they produce: the icosafold points of C2a and C2b, the points of C3 with 3600 symmetries (where the green order-3 girdle ends on the cross rings; C3 is named after the shape at these points and the rest of its range is subsymmetrical, like C2), and the six points where the light purple axes meet the domain boundary (the C2a and C2b icosafold points again, two F3 points on the face β₄ = 0, and a B1 point on the main ring).
  • Rings take the colour of the shape each stretch produces. Solid lines are exactly traced segments (T1, E2), drawn end to end; dashed lines are their copies under the extra half-turn that maps the group to itself but is not in H4, so they give the same polytope elsewhere in the cell; T2 (outlined, faint fill) is the exact regions traced in the mirrors, with dashed copies.

Renumbering

    T1 and T2 keep their numbers. C2, the subsymmetrical icosafold icosidodecaswirlchoron, sits at exact points that are not golden, so it is placed from the cross-ring analysis rather than sampled.
