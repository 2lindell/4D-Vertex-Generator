H3●I2(10) Symmetry Domain

A map of every isogonal polychoron the H3●I2(10) swirlprism group produces from a single seed. Each point of one fundamental domain, half of a 600-cell's tetrahedral cell, is a seed; it is coloured by the polychoron its 1200 images make. Regions, walls, lines and points of the domain are labelled with the wiki's names where they exist, and the views below show the same map from several fundamental domains.

Hover a point for its shape, golden weights and seed; click it to pin those details here and copy the seed. Click a row in the key to show only that shape, Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) to add or remove more, and click the only selected row again to show everything.

Key

Counts are symmetry classes of cells, faces and edges, written the way the wiki writes them. The example seed can be pasted into the generator's Coordinates mode.

The domain

  • The group is the swirlprism group H3●I2(10): 1200 rotations of the 3-sphere inside the H4 group of the 600-cell. A seed's 1200 images are the vertices of an isogonal polychoron, and two seeds give the same polychoron when one is an image of the other, so one fundamental domain holds every shape once.
  • The default view is half of one tetrahedral cell of the 600-cell (corners V₁, V₂, V₃ and M34 = β (0, 0, 1, 1), the midpoint of the edge V₃V₄), in gnomonic projection: great circles are straight lines and great spheres are planes. Points are written in barycentric weights β of the cell. The shaded top face is the H4 mirror β₃ = β₄ that splits the cell; the half-turn fixing the cell swaps V₁↔V₂ and V₃↔V₄, so the other half repeats this one, and seeds in the splitting face are also drawn at their copy there. The picture is upside down so that face is on top. The half cannot be halved again: the mirror β₁ = β₂ is not a symmetry, and the two quarters it separates hold different shapes.
  • Points are seeds with golden-integer weights, every uniform H4 seed, and extra samples where the golden grid is sparse; each is coloured by the polychoron it produces.
  • An extra half-turn Q, not in H4, maps the group to itself. A seed and its Q-image give congruent polychora, so most shapes appear twice in the domain; the group with Q has 2400 rotations.

Views

  • The half-cell (and its two quarters β₁ ≥ β₂ and β₁ ≤ β₂, to see inside).
  • The M34 dodecahedron: the points of the 3-sphere nearer to M34 than to the rest of its 120-point orbit, a cell of the dual 120-cell. The ten rotations fixing M34 turn it onto itself, so everything appears ten times about the vertical 5-fold axis (the main ring). A tenth of it is a fundamental domain: the upper half cut to the 72° wedge between two horizontal half-turn axes, so its edges at M34 lie on the main ring and two cross rings.
  • The V1 dodecahedron: the same picture around the 600-cell vertex V1 = β (1, 0, 0, 0). Its faces are H4 mirrors, and the 15 mirrors through V1 cut it into 120 whole H4 chambers in 12 orbits of 10. 12 whole H4 chambers is a fundamental domain made of one chamber from each orbit: the single convex solid with the most chamber edges on significant rings (8, with 14 faces shared between chambers), unique up to the rotations fixing V1. Chamber edges are drawn faintly and the faces shown are its outer boundary. It keeps regions more connected than the half-cell: 76 region pieces against 107, with F2 and F3 each in one piece.
  • Every region in one piece: a fundamental domain in which each region is as few pieces as possible. Each piece of a region in the half-cell is moved by a group element to meet the region's other pieces across the half-cell's faces. 90% of the seeds stay put, so the domain is the half-cell (its edges drawn faintly) with the moved pieces as bulges, mostly F3. 11 regions are one piece: F2, F3, X12, X16, X30, X32, X37, X58, X61, X68 and X71. The other 26 stay in 2 to 4 pieces, whose nearest contacts all cross another class. Connections are read from 7800 classified seeds (shown as dots) and checked by classifying points along them, so sparse regions may join more than shown. The grey surface is an approximate outline wrapped around the seeds; click a region's row to see its own outline.
  • Each view keeps its own camera, so ticking boxes never moves the picture.

Names and markers

  • The letter gives the vertex count and whether the wiki names the shape: A 120 named, B 240 named, C 600 named, D 600 unnamed, E 1200 named, F 1200 unnamed, T 1200 transitional, each numbered in the wiki's order. Shapes not in the wiki list are Y (600) and X (1200). C2a and C2b are the two subsymmetrical icosafold icosidodecaswirlchoron ranges on the cross rings, told apart by their counts.
  • Marker shape shows the vertex count: circles 1200, diamonds 600, squares 120 or 240. Large outlined markers are uniform H4 seeds, labelled by the shape they produce. Every named shape has its own colour, darkest for shapes on a line, lighter on a wall and lightest for regions; unlisted shapes are grey, in the same steps. Click a row in the key to show only that shape; Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) more rows to show several at once, with their wall patches, lines and outlines.
  • "Where" says where a shape lives: a region, a wall (plane) or line, or a point or curve. X shapes that only live on a wall, line or point are listed separately as transitional walls, lines and points, each grouped by where they lie.
  • "Boundaries" lists the shapes found on a shape's boundary, by what they fill: the shapes past either end of a traced line, the line shapes on the edges and the shapes at the corners of the exact wall patches, the regions on either side of those patches, and the transitional shapes whose nudged samples fall into a region. A shape whose surroundings are only partly traced has a partial list.
  • ✕ marks exact points that are not golden, in the colour of their shape: the icosafold points of C2a and C2b; the points of C3 with 3600 symmetries, where the green girdle meets the cross rings (C3 is named after the shape there; the rest of its range is subsymmetrical); the points where the purple axes meet the domain's boundary; and the points where a purple axis passes through a transitional shape (X1, X7 and X78).

Lines and axes

  • Rings are great circles fixed by rotations of the group: the main ring along the edge V₃V₄ and the cross rings. They take the colour of the shape each stretch produces.
  • Purple axes are the axes of the half-turns in the coset of Q. A seed on one is its own copy, and its polychoron has 2400 symmetries. The one from the β₂ = β₃ cross ring to C2b is one of Bowers' 30 ghost girdles with skew 20-gonal symmetry.
  • The green line is an order-3 ghost girdle: the axis of an order-3 rotation of a 3600-element group containing the swirlprism group. Seeds on it give X12 polychora with 3600 symmetries; it is one of Bowers' 20 such girdles. A search of every fixed circle of H4, its Q-coset and this group finds no other line with more than 1200 symmetries.
  • Transitional shapes (T1, T2 and the X walls and lines): T1 and E2 are exactly traced lines; T2 is three exact patches in the mirrors β₁ = β₃, β₂ = β₃ and β₁ = β₂, each with a spidrox corner; select a transitional shape's row in the key to see its patches of wall or its segments of line. The named wall shapes F1, F4 and F5 are traced exactly: each patch is a polygon in its wall bounded by exact lines (F5 also by a conic, along which the shape is X24), with corners and edges in its hover text. F1 lies in the mirrors β₁ = β₂ and β₁ = β₃, F4 in the faces β₁ = 0 and β₂ = 0, and F5 in the mirror β₂ = β₄; their dashed copies lie on further walls, two of them tilted.
  • Throughout, solid lines are the loci themselves and dashed lines are their copies under Q, which give the same polychora elsewhere in the domain.

Regular cells and faces

  • Regular pentagonal prisms (pink) lie on straight lines from M34 on the faces β₁ = 0 and β₂ = 0 and the mirror β₁ = β₂: β₃ = β₁ + β₄ and β₃ = β₂ + β₄ (F4, ending at spidrox), and β₁ = β₂ = (β₃ − β₄)/φ (F1). At β ∝ (2, 3, 2, 2−φ) the prism lines end where each prism merges with ten neighbouring cells into one 20-vertex cell.
  • Regular pentagonal antiprisms (brown) also lie on straight lines from M34: β₁ = φ²β₂, β₃ = β₁ + β₄ (F1) and β₂ = φ²β₁, β₃ = β₂ + β₄ (E1). Their copies run from the V points along the cell's edges and into the cell. A short piece inside X32, from the X19 point (2φ², 1, φ², φ²) to its own copy (3+2φ, 1, 2+φ, 1+φ), ends where each regular antiprism merges with another antiprism and five tetrahedra into a cupola-shaped cell. Spidrox (1, φ², φ², 0) is the one point where regular prisms and regular antiprisms are both cells.
  • Regular decagon faces (black): a decagon face is two regular pentagons, orbits of a 5-fold rotation that fixes a plane, lying in one plane; that happens on the X19 and X26 walls, and the decagon is regular where the two pentagons are turned exactly 36° apart. Both conditions are linear in the seed, so these are exact straight lines: one through M34 in the splitting mirror, and its copy from V1 to β ∝ (2, 1, 2, 1). ✕ marks where each decagon's cell is an untwisted pentagonal cupola, its pentagon lined up with the decagon: β ∝ (φ², 1, 1, 1), and its copy (φ², 1, 2, 1).
  • 2400 fundamental domain shows one fundamental domain of the group with Q: the points closer to a centre on the E2 line, β ∝ (2, 2, 2, 1) (black dot), than to any of its 2399 images. It has 13 corners and 10 faces and half the half-cell's volume; its four purple faces each fold onto themselves across a purple axis.
