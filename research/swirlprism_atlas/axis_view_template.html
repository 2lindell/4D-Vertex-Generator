Around a 2400-symmetry axis

The shapes round one of the axes of the extra half-turns (the 2400-element group's elements outside the swirlprism group), layer by layer out from the axis, each shown once and exploded so each stands apart from its neighbours. The axis is the purple line, moved with X38 (which runs along it) so it still lies along X38's edge there; the view is gnomonic, as in the domain page, centred on the axis.

  • Each shape is shown once, with only the extent its seeds need once. Of the copies of a shape, one is shown, picked one shape at a time: first the copy that bounds, or lies on, a shape already shown round an edge they share (so the shapes round an edge follow on one from the next: round B1, X38, X68, X99, X61, F4, X49, F1, F3), then the copy beside most shapes shown, then the one nearest the axis. A shape a symmetry of the 2400-element group carries onto itself is cut to a part no symmetry repeats: X38, C2a and B1 by the axis's half-turn, and F3, X16, C2b, X1, X12, X30, X7, X32 and X78 by other half-turns. Of the two halves of a cut shape, the one kept is the one meeting the corner A1 shown, if either does (so X38, B1 and F3 meet at that A1), then the one touching more of the shapes shown beside it. (D1, D2, D3 and C3 also have a second symmetry, but it turns each about itself, holding it point by point, so they are shown whole.)   • The view is exploded so that every shape keeps its place against its neighbours. The corners spread out as if the whole picture were scaled up about the axis, so each sits beyond the ends of its edges. Every other shape is set off the shapes on its boundary: an edge off its corners along itself (so C2a slides out from C6), the walls and regions round an edge off it in the directions they truly lie in from it, in their true order and over the arc they truly span (round E1 they span 311°, round B1 187°, round C2a 55°), opened out only where neighbours crowd: to at least 8° apart, or up to 30° round an edge with few shapes, the shapes of the earliest layer round it keeping their true directions and the rest swinging out from them; each wall or region is also turned about the edge it opens out most round (of its own layer or nearer the axis), so the shapes round an edge fan out from it rather than standing parallel. Round an edge the symmetry turns about itself three or more times (B1, five times), the shapes shown fill one of the sectors that turn repeats, and they are shown as one group there: in the order they lie round it, spread evenly over the sector, each turned to stand straight out from the edge and set just off it, placed off that edge alone.
