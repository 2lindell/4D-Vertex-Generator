Around a 2400-symmetry axis

The shapes nearest one of the axes of the extra half-turns (the 2400-element group's elements outside the swirlprism group), each shown once and moved out from the axis so each stands apart. The axis is the purple line; the view is gnomonic, as in the domain page, centred on the axis.

drag to turn · scroll to zoom · hover a shape
  • Each shape is shown with only the extent its seeds need once: a shape the 2400-element group carries onto itself is cut to the part no symmetry repeats (X38, turned onto itself by the axis's half-turn: its half on one side of the axis); of a shape and its copies, one is shown (X68's two sides of X38, which the half-turn carries onto each other: one side; X99, X68's other wall, one copy bounding each side: the one bounding the side shown).
  • Their edges (E1, C2a, B1) and corners (A1, C6) are shown the same way: C2a and B1 cross the axis and the half-turn turns each end for end, so each is shown by its half; E1 and the corners come in pairs the half-turn swaps, so one of each is shown.
  • The view is exploded about the axis: the whole picture is scaled up about the axis's point at the centre of the view while every shape keeps its own size (each moved so its middle, of a wall's area or an edge's length, goes out from that point). So every shape keeps its place against its neighbours, with a gap: a corner sits just beyond the ends of its edges, an edge just off its wall's side. A region moves with the walls bounding it, and each of those walls then moves straight off it, away from its volume: X38 and X99 enclose X68 like a lens, so they stand apart on either side of it. (The walls curve round the axis, so moving the region out from the axis would slide it along them instead.) The camera turns round the axis. Walls and edges are solid; a region is shown by its shell, the walls bounding it, see-through.
